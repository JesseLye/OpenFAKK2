# Status by area

Where each part of the engine stands, in detail. The [README](../../README.md) has the summary;
[`known-differences.md`](../known-differences.md) has what was read from the original
executable for each, and what still differs.

Playable, not finished. The game starts with the retail intro and main menu, and New
Game runs the opening cinematic, `fakkhouse` and on into `training`, with weapons and
their effects, the HUD and inventory, sound, music and dialog. Games save and load in
the retail format. Most of it has been checked side by side against the retail game
(see [How OpenFAKK2 is made and checked](08-development.md)). Not yet: a playthrough of all 30 levels, and any
platform other than 32-bit Windows.

| Area | State |
| --- | --- |
| Engine base | ioquake3 `87a99d18`, rebranded. The dedicated server (`openfakk2ded`) and the client (`openfakk2`, GL1 renderer only) build. The client starts at the menu or with `map <name>`, runs a local game, and has a console and a map viewer |
| Game data | Retail pk3s mount; BSP v12 collision data loads for all 30 retail maps |
| Game modules | `gamex86.dll` / `cgamex86.dll` build from Ritual's 1.02 SDK source (x86, MSVC). Under the retail engine the rebuilt `gamex86.dll` matches retail console output at map spawn on four maps. Under OpenFAKK2 both the rebuilt and the retail `cgamex86.dll` load and draw |
| Engine ↔ game interface | Server side (`GAME_API_VERSION 4`): the game module loads, initialises and spawns maps. With `developer 1`, the lines the game prints while spawning a map are identical to the retail engine's on all 30 retail maps. It also runs the retail `gamex86.dll` unmodified, with the same result. With a headless test player (`sv_testplayer 1`), level scripts and cinematics run; ten seconds after the player enters, level variables match the retail game on all 29 comparable maps, and the state of every entity (up to 300 per map) matches on 7 maps and differs on 1 to 9 entities on the others, mostly by random choices that also vary between retail runs. See [`known-differences.md`](../known-differences.md). Client side (`CGAME_IMPORT_API_VERSION 3`): the client game module loads (the rebuilt one or the retail `cgamex86.dll`), initialises from the game state, receives snapshots and the sounds the game starts, and draws the scene and its 2D layer, with the level's far plane and its fog as the retail renderer draws them. Its collision, TIKI and alias queries are answered; sounds play through Quake III's sound system with FAKK2's volumes, minimum distances and attenuation (from `global/sound0.txt`), including the `.mp3` dialog (decoded with dr_mp3), the level's music follows the game's moods as the retail engine's does, and the level's reverb triggers set an EAX reverb (OpenAL Soft's EFX, shipped with the build, or a Freeverb from the same presets in the software mixer) that the retail engine only produced on an EAX card; the lip-sync data (the `.lip` file beside each dialog sound) is read and handed to the module; the camera-look offsets are a stub. Tested on `zoo` and `training` only |
| TIKI models (`.tik` `.tan` `.skb` `.ska`) | Definitions, animations and skeletons load for the server's and the client game module's queries; bounds and radius match the retail engine's cached values on 742 of 743 models. Tags are placed as the retail engine places them, with bone controllers, the legs/torso split and cross-blending. The renderer draws vertex-animated models (`.tan`) and skeletal ones (`.skb` with `.ska`) in whichever animation and frame the game asks for, interpolating between frames; bones are composed as the retail engine composes them for tags, split between the legs and torso animations, cross-blended from the animation being left, and turned by the entity's bone controllers as the retail renderer does. Models are lit from the map's light grid at FAKK2's spacing, from the centre of their bounds, with the retail engine's rules for the minimum light, light offsets and dynamic lights; the training room's door button matches the retail capture. The player and the actors (the `betterlighting` flag) are lit per vertex from a six-sided light cube built from the map's entity lights, its sun and ambient, as the retail renderer builds it; Julie in `training` matches the retail capture within a unit or two. Models, and the ones the map compiler baked into the map, drop vertexes along their collapse maps as they get smaller on screen, as the retail renderer does (`r_lodscale`, `r_lodbias`, the definitions' `lod_scale` and `lod_bias`); `r_showlod` writes each model's kept and total triangles over it as retail does. |
| Map viewer | `viewmap <name>` in the client draws a retail map with a fly camera (`viewpos`, `viewsky`, `viewstop`, normal movement keys): `.ftx` textures, lightmaps, curves, FAKK2's shader script extensions, sky portals, the far plane and its fog as worldspawn sets them, and the models the map places (in the first frame of their idle animation). All 30 retail maps load and draw, fog volumes included. Not drawn: anything a level script or the game sets up. [`known-differences.md`](../known-differences.md) says which parts of the picture have been compared with the retail game's |
| Weapons and effects | Every weapon fires. Muzzle flashes, impact puffs, bullet holes, beams, explosions, smoke and soot come from the weapons' TIKI effect blocks; decals are fitted to the collision model as retail fits them; `.spr` sprites are laid out by their shader's `spriteGen` and drawn after everything else, in retail's order; sword, axe and soul sucker swipes are drawn; dynamic lights light the world along retail's lighting pass. Compared weapon by weapon with the retail game through screenshot probes on `zoo` |
| Sound | OpenAL Soft (shipped with the build) or Quake III's software mixer. Loudness and panning follow retail's Miles provider, measured from recordings of both games; looping weapon sounds (the uzi's rattle) repeat their loop block while the trigger is held; the music follows the game's moods and keeps playing while the window is dragged |
| Saved games | `savegame`, `loadgame` and the load and save menu, in the retail engine's file format: the retail install's saves load with either game module, and the game is autosaved as "Starting <level>" 1.8 s into each level. `gamemap` carries the player's inventory to the next level, as the retail engine does. A save loaded in a new process matches the saved game on 132 of 133 entities and on all but one level variable on `zoo` (both differences come from the game module). See [`known-differences.md`](../known-differences.md) |
| `.urc` UI | A fresh implementation (`cl_urc.c`) loads every `ui/*.urc`, draws the HUD (health, water, the one- or two-handed weapon menu, the boss bar) as the retail client chooses it, draws the game's centre and location prints with the Ritual fonts, plays the retail intro at startup (the Ritual and Gathering logos fading in and out to the title theme, skipped by Escape) and then shows the main menu, which Escape also brings up, and the retail loading screen (the pulsing `textures/menu/loading` picture on black) from a map load's start to its first snapshot. The system cursor works the menus: buttons highlight, click (with their hover and click sounds) and run their commands, check boxes toggle their cvar; Escape closes a menu, and console commands (`pushmenu`, `popmenu`, `listmenus`, `centerprint`, `locationprint`, ...) drive it too. Sliders drag, pulldowns open their lists, labels show their linked cvar through their `linkstring` names, the controls page's key bind list (`ui/bind.scr`) shows and rebinds keys, and the title theme plays at the menu. The menus are drawn as the retail widget library draws them (fonts, borders, slider arrows, check box captions, the bind list's pictures, scroll bars, the saved-game list with its picture, the GFX info text) and capture like retail's pixel for pixel at 640x480; the load and save menu loads, saves and deletes games. The level script's screen fades and letterbox are drawn from the player state, as the retail engine drew them, and the prints sit where retail puts them (the intro's quote on black, the credits at the bottom right). FAKK2's attack, use and sneak buttons reach the game, and the inventory bar (`global/inventory.scr`: the number keys, the wheel, and the attack keys to equip the highlighted item) works as the retail client's does, with the items' models turning in their frames, their ammo bars, the wielded weapons in the HUD's windows, and an inventory screen on the `i` key. Fields and lists draw their text but cannot be edited. An "OpenFAKK2 Options" button on the video and audio menu opens a page of OpenFAKK2's own (`code/client/urc/`, built in) for what retail's menus lack: anti-aliasing, anisotropic filtering, the frame rate limit, field of view, HUD size, the widescreen view and HUD, vertical sync, the controller, OpenAL and HRTF, a borderless window and demo recording; the video menu's resolution list has the desktop's size and every size the display has. The advanced menu's Notepad (`notepad`) is retail's text editor window, with its file picker |
| Lens flares | The sun's flare (worldspawn `sunflare`, through the sky portal) and the flares of `+lensflare` entities and lights are drawn as the retail renderer draws them, from `scripts/lensflare2.txt`, hidden behind the world by a trace. The intro's Lochnar and `training`'s shooting targets match the retail captures |
